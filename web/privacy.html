Privacy

Last updated 24 October 2026

type me it turns your speech into text on your Mac. Your speech, the text it becomes, and the history it keeps never leave the computer. This page lists what the app touches, what it stores, and the servers it talks to. It covers both versions of the app: the one downloaded from this site, and the one sold on the Mac App Store, which does less. Where they differ, the page says so.

What the app does with your speech

While you hold the dictation key the app records from the microphone: fn 🌐 in the version from this site, ⌥ space in the Mac App Store version. When you let go, the recording is transcribed on your Mac by the Parakeet Ultra speech model, running inside the app through FluidAudio. The audio is held in memory for the length of the transcription and then discarded, unless "keep the audio" is on in settings, in which case each recording is saved next to its dictation and deleted with it. Audio is never sent anywhere.

If Apple Intelligence is turned on in settings, the transcript is tidied up by Apple's on-device foundation model, through the foundationmodels framework. That model runs on your Mac. type me it does not use private cloud compute or any other Apple server for this step. Apple's own privacy policy governs Apple Intelligence itself.

The finished text is typed into whichever app has the cursor.

If "match what is behind it" is on in settings, the app reads an 8 by 8 pixel picture of the screen under the recording cloud to choose whether the cloud is white or dark. The pixels are averaged in memory and discarded. Nothing is stored or sent. macOS asks for screen recording because that is the only permission that allows any of the screen to be read, and it reminds you from time to time that the app has it. The option is off unless you turn it on.

If "read the screen for names and terms" is on in settings, the app takes a picture of the frontmost window when you start dictating and reads the text in it on your Mac, with Apple's vision framework, so the clean-up can spell names and terms as they appear on screen. The picture is discarded once read. The words are kept in memory for a short while, for another dictation into the same window, and are never written to disk. This also needs screen recording, and it is off unless you turn it on.

What the app stores on your Mac

Everything below lives in ~/library/application support/typemeit/, or for the Mac App Store version in the app's container, ~/library/containers/it.typeme.typemeit/data/library/application support/typemeit/, and in the app's preferences. You can open, copy or delete these files at any time.

  • history.json holds each dictation: the transcript, the Apple Intelligence version if there was one, any edit you made afterwards, the time, how long the recording and transcription took, and the app you dictated into (bundle identifier and app name). The version from this site also records the window title, and computes the insights tab from this file. You choose how many entries to keep in settings, including none, and you can delete any entry or all of them from the history tab.
  • recordings/ holds the audio of each dictation, only while "keep the audio" is on.
  • learned-words.json (version from this site) holds pairs of what the app heard and what you meant, gathered from your corrections, together with when each was learned and which dictation it came from. These improve future transcripts. Each one can be undone or removed from the custom words list.
  • models/ holds the downloaded speech model.
  • Voices/ (version from this site) exists only while "learn voices" is on in settings. For each transcribed meeting it holds numbers that describe each speaker's voice, and no audio. The app compares them to suggest names for speakers the meeting did not name. Turning the setting off deletes the folder; deleting a meeting deletes its voices.
  • Preferences hold your settings, such as the hotkey, the history limit and whether the app checks for updates.

The app writes diagnostic messages to the macOS unified log, which stays on your Mac and is managed by the system. If "debug logs" is on in settings, it also writes what each dictation and paste did, including the transcript, to ~/library/logs/typemeit/debug.log, which settings can delete.

How learning reads your corrections

This is the version from this site only; the Mac App Store version does not read other apps. After a dictation is typed, the app watches the text field it typed into for a short while, through macOS accessibility, so that if you fix a word it can learn the correction. It only reads the field it just typed into. It does not read fields in password managers or terminals, and it does nothing while macOS secure input is active, which is the state a password field puts the system into.

What the app connects to

The version from this site makes two kinds of network request, and the Mac App Store version only the first. Neither carries your speech, your text or your history. What the other end sees is your ip address and the request itself.

  • The speech model, downloaded once during onboarding from this app's GitHub releases page. GitHub sees an ordinary download request from your ip address.
  • Update checks (version from this site), made by the Sparkle framework against the app's GitHub releases page. By default the app checks on a schedule Sparkle manages. The request includes the app version and macOS version so the right update can be offered. Nothing else about your Mac is sent. You can turn automatic checks off in settings, and check by hand from the menu instead. The Mac App Store version is updated by the App Store.

There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising. The app makes no other network requests.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Microphone, to hear you while the dictation key is held.
  • Accessibility, in the version from this site to notice the fn 🌐 key being pressed and released in any app, to type the text where the cursor is, to read the title of the window you dictated into, and to read back your corrections as described above. The Mac App Store version uses it only to paste the text where the cursor is.
  • Screen recording, only if you turn on one of the two screen options above.

Each is granted in System Settings and can be withdrawn there. Without the microphone the app cannot dictate. Without accessibility the version from this site cannot see the fn 🌐 key, type, attribute dictations to an app, or learn from corrections, and the Mac App Store version offers to copy the text instead of pasting it.

The website

typeme.it is a static page served by Cloudflare. Cloudflare records the requests it serves, including your ip address, under its own privacy policy. The page loads the dm mono typeface from google fonts, which means google receives the request for the font file. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics. The download button links to the app's GitHub releases, so GitHub handles the download. Purchases on the Mac App Store are handled by Apple, and type me it receives no information about who bought the app.

Children

The app and site are not directed at children under 13 and do not knowingly collect information from them.

Your rights

type me it holds no data about you, so there is nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete on your behalf. Every record the app makes is a file on your Mac that you control, and deleting the app's application support folder, or the Mac App Store version's container, removes all of it.

Changes

If the app starts touching anything new, this page changes first and the date at the top moves. The history of this page is in the public repository.
